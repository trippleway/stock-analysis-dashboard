# The six-layer AI value chain

## Why six layers?

Traditional stock classifications, such as GICS sectors, do not capture where value actually moves in an AI cycle.

This system reclassifies names through the lens of AI commercialization, so the investment logic is easier to follow:

- **Where the money goes**: from CapEx (Layer 1-2) to platforms (Layer 3-4) to applications (Layer 5-6)
- **Commercial progress**: each layer is at a different stage
- **Risk profile**: cyclical risk at the bottom, competitive risk at the top

---

## Layer 1 Infrastructure

### Definition

The physical hardware and components required to train and run AI.

### Subgroups

- **AI accelerators**: NVDA, AMD, AVGO
- **Memory**: MU, WDC (HBM is critical to AI)
- **Wafer manufacturing**: TSM, ASML, AMAT, LRCX, KLAC
- **AI servers**: SMCI, DELL
- **Cooling and power**: VRT
- **Edge compute**: MRVL, ARM

### Investment logic

- Benefits from the AI CapEx cycle
- Deep moats (leading-edge process, patents)
- Highly cyclical (the semiconductor cycle)

### What to track

- Semiconductor inventory cycle
- Capital spending at the four large clouds
- TSMC utilization

---

## Layer 2 Cloud & compute

### Definition

Cloud platforms that sell the compute used to train and run AI.

### Subgroups

- **Hyperscale cloud**: MSFT (Azure), GOOGL (GCP), AMZN (AWS)
- **Enterprise cloud**: ORCL, IBM
- **AI-specific cloud**: CRWV
- **CDN and edge**: NET, AKAM

### Investment logic

- Direct beneficiary of the surge in AI demand
- Scale and customer stickiness
- Heavy capital-spending pressure

### What to track

- AI-related revenue growth at each cloud
- Capital-spending guidance (capex guidance)
- Customer concentration

---

## Layer 3 Foundation models

### Definition

Listed companies that build their own foundation models or hold a strategic stake in one.

### Subgroups

- **Open models**: META (Llama)
- **China domestic**: BIDU (ERNIE)
- **On-device AI**: AAPL
- **Vertical AI**: TSLA (self-driving), PLTR (defense)

### Investment logic

- The model is the hub of AI
- A chance to build a platform effect
- The highest regulatory risk

### What to track

- Model rankings (MMLU, HELM)
- Developer adoption
- Sentiment in regulatory news

---

## Layer 4 Data platforms

### Definition

The data infrastructure and tools that AI commercialization needs.

### Subgroups

- **Data warehouses**: SNOW, MDB, ESTC
- **Observability**: DDOG
- **AI platforms**: AI, PATH, SOUN
- **DevOps for AI**: GTLB, CFLT
- **AI security**: S

### Investment logic

- A picks-and-shovels position, not a bet on one model
- Customer adoption is still accelerating
- The moat comes from data network effects

### What to track

- ARR growth
- Net Revenue Retention (NRR)
- Share of customers that are AI-related

---

## Layer 5 AI applications

### Definition

Companies that add AI capability to an existing SaaS product.

### Subgroups

- **CRM and sales**: CRM (Einstein), HUBS
- **IT services**: NOW
- **Design tools**: ADBE (Firefly), U
- **Developer collaboration**: TEAM
- **HR and finance**: WDAY, INTU
- **Commerce**: SHOP
- **Ad tech**: TTD, APP
- **Communications**: ZM

### Investment logic

- Commercialization started to break out in 2025
- Ability to charge an AI premium
- Competition is intense and the moat is still being tested

### What to track

- AI-related ARR
- Customer adoption
- Pricing power (gross margin)

---

## Layer 6 Consumer & content

### Definition

Consumer products, content, and entertainment platforms that AI is changing.

### Subgroups

- **Music streaming**: SPOT (including SONY music content)
- **Video streaming**: NFLX, DIS, WBD
- **Social and content**: META, PINS, SNAP, RDDT
- **Games**: RBLX
- **Education**: DUOL

### Investment logic

- AI cuts both ways for entertainment
- Personalized recommendations versus the threat from generative AI
- A direct link to the job-search story (Sony Music)

### What to track

- How AI changes user retention
- How the content cost structure changes
- Copyright issues
